# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

huckepack is a website that collects and visualises data about official long-distance hiking trails worldwide, focused on well-known thru-hiking routes: one page per trail plus comparisons across trails.

**Mission (docs/mission.md): huckepack helps outdoor enthusiasts find the long-distance trail that suits them, and plan the journey with the data that matters on the way, from official and open sources, named on every page and comparable across trails.** Base every decision on it: before building a feature, adding a trail or data source or changing a design, check it against the mission's checklist ("Deciding on something new"): does it help find a trail or plan the journey, which planning question does it answer, is there an official or open source every figure can name, can it be shown the same way on every trail, does it keep the pages calm, accessible and fast. Say so when a request doesn't fit the mission, and propose what would. docs/scope.md holds decisions and open questions (record new decisions there), docs/data-sources.md lists sources with their licences, docs/roadmap.md ranks candidate trails and features (update it when one is built). **Building a trail follows docs/trail-building.md**, the checklist from picking a trail to going live (sources, record rules, pipeline, data check, done, publish, unattended runs).

The large Geofabrik extracts live outside the synced project folder, in `~/.cache/huckepack/` (`osm-extracts/`; `dem/` held the Copernicus terrain tiles until 2026-11-07, no longer used; since 2026-11-06, before in `data/osm/extracts/` and `data/dem/`, which Proton Drive synced; `HUCKEPACK_CACHE`, `HUCKEPACK_OSM_EXTRACTS` or `HUCKEPACK_DEM` name other folders); the extracts are kept to 8 GB (`HUCKEPACK_EXTRACTS_MAX_GB`), the least recently used dropped first. The small per-trail corridors stay in `data/osm/corridors/`. Steps share a trail's cache manifest (`data/raw/<slug>/manifest.json`), written under a file lock and merged (`Cache._save`), since the photos step runs as its own process beside the others. OSM data come from Geofabrik extracts read locally (default), or from the public Overpass API with `OSM_SOURCE=overpass` (`OVERPASS_URL` picks a mirror). Hosts a full run needs in the sandbox: download.geofabrik.de, s3.opengeohub.org, esa-worldcover.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com, openmeteo.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com, router.project-osrm.org, plus the track's host.

Inside Claude Code's sandbox, pip needs `PIP_CACHE_DIR="$TMPDIR/pip-cache"`. Python downloads verify certificates through the OS trust store (`truststore`); never disable verification.

## Architecture

- `data/trails/<slug>.yaml`: the trail database, one file per trail; the file name is the URL slug. `src/content.config.ts` is its schema (Zod): every figure names a `source` id from the trail's `sources`, and the build fails on an unknown id. A source's `retrieved: null` means its figures are not yet checked; the page says "not yet verified". `notes` holds discrepancies between sources and open questions; they are for us, not shown on the page.
- `src/lib/trails.ts`: derived figures, never stored: totals from the main stages (`kind: main`; approaches and variants are excluded; they feed the stages table and difficulty), the days (`trailDays()`: the record's `days` only, never derived; the user's rule of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md "Where days come from": the operator's, else a trusted second source's, else one per main stage of the operator's stage list, each record stating its `days.basis` (`operator`, `secondary`, `stages`; the schema checks a `stages` count); a trail that fits none has no days and a "Days (date): …" note, shows no Days figure, and drops out of the search's How long filter only once a length is picked; the Days popover names the source, "one per stage of <operator>'s stage list" for `stages`), the averages per day (`perDay(t, d)`, the user's rule 4 of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md; replacing the averages per stage, `perStage()`, issue #148, now removed: the trail's length as the page shows it, `trailLength()`, over its days, `trailDays()`, and the total ascent, `trailClimb()`, likewise, nothing else, for every trail with days, with or without stages; a range of days gives a range, the larger km from the fewest days, one value where min = max; km to whole numbers, a tenth under 10 km, metres to 10 m; no days, no figure (today the Országos Kéktúra); `hardest` keeps the day at the fewest days on the unrounded length for difficulty's Effort on a trail without stages, while trails with stages keep Effort on their longest stage), the trail's length (`trailLength(t, d)`, the user's decision of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md: our measurement, `measuredLength()`, the main route's length on the full matched OSM line, the derived `measured` from pipeline/routes.py (main stages plus `outAndBack` side trips twice, variants and approaches left out), in whole kilometres (`exact` keeps the tenth for the popover), used where the route it measures is at least 95 % matched overall, weighted by stage length (`measuredCover()`, `MEASURED_MIN_COVER`; only the West Island Way, 94 %, falls short); else the provider's figure, `providerLength()`: the first stated total in the record, else the stage sum; one total everywhere: the key figure, title, description, link-preview image, search and home-page cards, the A–Z list, the season planner, sorting and totals; the Length popover gives ours to a tenth, how it was measured, the provider's figure as "Official" with its source, and "Keep in mind" where they differ by over 10 %, or, falling back, why; the averages per day take this length too (rule 4), while the stage table and the difficulty's stage figures stay on the provider's lengths; "about" only for a provider's figure flagged `approx`), the total ascent and descent (`trailClimb(t, d)`, the user's decision of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md: our terrain model's estimate, `terrainClimb()`, GEDTM30 along our map line summed per main stage with the 10 m threshold, each stage to 10 m, exactly as `stageEstimates()` has them, or over the whole route for a trail without stages, `routeEstimate()`; `estimate: true`, `how` `CLIMB_HOW`, shown with ≈ everywhere as an estimate; behind the same gate as the length, at least 95 % of the route matched (`measuredCover()`, `MEASURED_MIN_COVER`) and a profile reaching every main stage; else the provider's figure, `providerClimb()`: the stated pair, `ascent` and `descent` from one source, else the main stages' sums when every stage states both; else, where no source states one, the estimate all the same (today the West Island Way); one figure everywhere: the key figure, search cards and the "least climbing per km" sort, the link-preview image, the home page's metres climbed, the averages per day (`perDay()`, the total over the days) and the difficulty's climbing per km; the popover's facts come from `climbFacts()`: "This trail" (estimated), "How", the provider's figure as "Official" with its source, and "Keep in mind" where they differ by over 10 % (the climbs come from 100 m samples on every trail, `stageClimbs`), or, falling back, why; the stage table keeps each stage's stated climbs and the DIN times stay on each stage's own figures; side trips walked there and back (`outAndBack`, the W Trek's and the O Circuit's valleys) count both ways, as the measured length counts them twice: each one's climbs one way (the derived `sideTrips`), up and down, added to both the ascent and the descent, and the How row says "side trips walked there and back counted both ways"; GLO-30, the surface model used until 2026-11-07, read treetops, 15–60 % high in forest) and the comparable length (`compareLength()`: the same figure as `trailLength()`, kept as the name search sorting, climbing per km and the home page's total kilometres use), and the SAC grade for filtering (stated; converted from CAI with `CAI_TO_SAC`; or, when no grade is published, our estimate in the record's `difficulty.estimated` with a `basis` sentence from the source's description, shown in the popover). Every place that explains the grade says `SAC_NOTE`: grades are translated into the SAC scale to compare trails, but stay an estimate. Each result carries its source.
- `pipeline/`: Python, downloads and processes source data into `data/raw/<trail>/` (committed cache, with `manifest.json` retrieval times; official GPX tracks are copyrighted and git-ignored, so a fresh clone needs `--refresh` once; never commit them, the repo is public; a stage's OSM ways are cached by its number alone (`osm-stage-<n>-main.json`), so when a stage's `end`, `relation` or track changes, delete that stage's cache and the next one's before rerunning: the user's decision of 2026-11-02 is to keep caches downloaded once, not keyed by the track) and writes derived data. The site build never downloads. `pipeline/routes.py` builds `public/routes/<slug>.geojson`: one simplified LineString per stage (number, kind, coverage) of OpenStreetMap geometry, matched by `pipeline/osm.py` to the stage's `track` (GPX). The matcher fetches OSM ways within 200 m of the track (from `osm.source()`, see OSM data below; Overpass is queried as padded boxes over 1 km pieces of the track, `osm.boxes()`, since the polyline `around` can silently match nothing; an answer without ways is an error, never cached; cached as `osm-stage-*.json`) and takes the cheapest path from start to end, where a way costs its length × (1 + (distance from track / 15 m)²). Where the track turns back on itself (out to a summit or viewpoint and back the same way: the points 150 m before and after lie together, `turnarounds()`), each leg is matched on its own and the legs chained, since the cheapest path would skip the spur (the Hexen-Stieg's Brocken, the Alpe-Adria-Trail's stages 7 and 30); the same for a short loop out to a viewpoint and back on a parallel path (the track returns within 100 m of where it left after 0.6–4 km, `LOOP_GAP_M`; split at the loop's farthest point; the Moselsteig's stage 3, the Forststeig's stage 5, the Pieterpad's last stage over the Sint-Pietersberg, 3.4 km; 3 km before 2026-11-03, which no other cached track reaches), and a stage that is itself a loop, ending where it began (the South West Coast Path's stage 49 round the Isle of Portland, 22 km), split at its farthest point, since the cheapest path from its start to its end would be empty. A split needs an OSM node within 30 m of the turn, so a stray track point can't force a detour. Where the ways near the track don't connect at all (the Sauerland-Waldroute's stage 17 crosses the Ruhr at Bestwig where OSM maps the driveway as removed), the matcher bridges the shortest gap near the track, up to 100 m (`MAX_BRIDGE_M`; way id 0 in `routeWays`, unknown surface), prints it, and the gap goes in the trail's notes; trails whose ways connect are untouched. At most `MAX_BRIDGES` (6) per stage, shortest first; a stage's `maxBridgeM` allows a longer one where OSM maps a crossing as under construction (the Cotswold Way's stage 7 bridges 133 m over the rebuilt A417; docs/scope.md), or a river ferry the trail takes (the Pieterpad's over the Rhine and the Maas), and its `maxBridges` more of them where small unconnected bits of OSM near the track use up the six before the long one. The GPX only guides the choice; none of its geometry is published. A trail without stages by design (`stages.none`, issue #204: the Wonderland Trail) is one internal segment over the whole route (`osm.stage_list()`: main stage "1", `whole`, to the finish; a loop's finish is its start), guided by `route.track` (one GPX) or the relation's chained line, a loop's never cut, a one-way trail's cut at the point nearest its finish (2026-11-06: the Lost Coast Trail's relation runs on past the BLM's Black Sands Beach); the other steps work on it unchanged, `pipeline.all`'s check compares the whole route's length, and the site ignores its stage number (no stages section, kilometre marks on the charts, averages per day from the suggested days, `perDay()`, Effort from an average day at the fewest days). Trails without GPX (Laugavegur) set `route.relation` (the OSM hiking relation) and an `end` point per stage: the relation's main ways (plus `route.alternatives`, ways with role alternative that close gaps) are chained from the start into one line, cut at the stage ends, and each piece guides the matcher like a track (a stage with `via` points as well as its `end`, where the official route takes the relation's alternative or excursion ways and OSM's main line shortcuts, follows the shortest way over all the relation's ways through them, `relation_path`; the Alta Via 1's stages 3, 4 and 6, 2026-11-04; with `route.branched` each stage is the shortest way over all the relation's ways to its `end` (`route.relation` can be a list, whose relations' ways are merged into one: the Kammweg's three sections, 2026-11-06) (through its `via` points where it has them, since 2026-11-06: a circuit back to where it began, the West Island Way's stage 1), and `route.joinM` joins way ends that stop that close to another way, e.g. refuges' spurs: the Carros de Foc's 7–50 m, 2026-11-04; a stage's own `joinM` does so for that stage only, where a larger one for every stage would open shortcuts (the West Island Way's relation is in three pieces: stage 1 joins 850 m across Kilchattan Bay, stage 4 the moorland's 160–220 m; the matcher then follows the real roads, though the stage's coverage counts the guide's straight jump as unmatched); `relation_line`, `split_at`; a round trip such as the Tour du Mont Blanc can chain either way round from its start, so the line is turned when the first stage's end comes after the second-last one's, `nearest_index`; a jump over `MAX_RELATION_GAP_M` ends the chain and the leftover ways are reported). Where OSM maps a trail as one relation per section (a superroute, e.g. the West Highland Way), each stage names its own `relation` instead (child relations included): chained from where the previous main stage ended, it guides that stage (cached as `osm-relation-<id>.json`). A stage's `relation` can also be a list (an OSM section and the start of the next, e.g. the Westweg's stage 9: section 9 and the western variant's relation), and with an `end` point the stage is cut out of its relations: they are chained from the end nearest the trail's start, and the piece from where the stage begins to the point nearest its `end` guides it (`piece()`; chaining from a point mid-relation could head the wrong way). One GPX for the whole trail (the Coast to Coast's) serves every stage: each stage names the same `track` and its `end` point, and the stage is the track's piece from where the previous main stage ended to the point nearest its `end` (the shared track is downloaded and read once, `track_of_url`); a GPX offered as a zip archive is unpacked on download (`unzipped_gpx()`, St Oswald's Way's section files, 2026-11-06), and a KML or KMZ (also a KMZ inside a zip) is read as its line strings in order (the Caminho de Cora Coralina's official track, 2026-11-06, #269); a GPX with several tracks is read as its first (the National Trail's file has the main route first, then two alternatives); a GPX route (`rtept`, the Cotswold Way's and the Camí de Cavalls' files) is read like a track; a track drawn from the finish (the Cotswold Way's, from Bath) is turned: it should run from where the stage before its first stage ended (or the trail's start) to its last stage's end (comparing with the trail's start and finish turned the Országos Kéktúra's section 15, which runs north-west). Several stages can share one section's track with their own `end` points (the Kéktúra's 59 days on its 27 section GPX files). A variant listed right after another variant chains from that one's end, so a run of variant stages (the Westweg's eastern 9–13, listed after main stage 8 where they branch off) follows on. A `track` can also be a tour page that carries its track as schema.org data (`"geo"` GeoShape `"line"`, as Outdooractive's pages do, e.g. the Alpe-Adria-Trail's touren.kaernten.at stage pages, whose GPX download needs a login); `track_points()` reads either (also the line as "lon,lat,height" triples, eifelsteig.de, and an `@type` list ["GeoCoordinates", "GeoShape"] with the start's coordinates before the line, hermannshoehen.teutoburgerwald.de, and the neusta tour pages' HTML-escaped `"polyline": "lon,lat lon,lat …"`, sauerland-waldroute.de, whose GPX download needs a licence key), and the cached copy keeps the `.gpx` name so it stays git-ignored. Where a stage cut out of its relations by `end` chains clearly less (over 1 km) from the end nearest the trail's start than from the relation's open end (a way end no other way continues from), the open end is used: on the Rothaarsteig's stages 6–8 a way in the middle lay nearer Brilon and the chain stopped after a few ways (`line_km`, `relation_line(..., from_open_end=True)`; the Westweg and the West Highland Way come out identical). The same rule (`relation_chain()`) holds for a stage relation without an `end`, chained from where the previous stage ended: the South West Coast Path's South Dorset Ridgeway variant passes just above Abbotsbury, where stage 47 ends, and its chain stopped after 4.9 of 27 km (the nine trails with such stages came out byte-identical, 2026-11-03). The OSM ways per stage are cached by stage (`osm-stage-<n>-<kind>.json`), not by track: delete a stage's cache when its guide line changes. Stage numbers can carry a route letter (the Goldsteig's N8–N23 and S8–S22); the pipeline keeps the main stages in the record's order (since 2026-11-07, for the New England Trail's "CT 5" … "MA 19", whose digits don't sort; before, by their digits, `osm.stage_order`, which gave the same order on every trail). A lift the stage rides (`stages.transfers` with `stage`, by cable car or funicular: the Adlerweg's stages 8, 12 and 17, whose official GPX runs along the lift) is cut out of the stage's guide (`walked_pieces()`): the pieces on either side are matched on their own and written as features of the same stage, the later ones with `part` (2, 3, …), which the steps add up per stage and never count as a stage start or end; the ride is a transfer feature drawn through its `via` points in the ride style. A stage that can't be walked in full (the Eifelsteig's stage 14 since a Kyll bridge was lost in the 2021 flood) gets `closed`: `at` (where it can be walked to), `since`, `reason`, `source` and the operator's recommended `detour`, leg by leg (`by: foot` with `via` points that keep the guide on the recommended path, matched to OSM like a track; `by: train` or `bus` with the OSM `relation` of the line, followed by the shortest way through its ways, `relation_path()`, since a railway's double tracks and junctions defeat chaining). The stage's track is cut at `at` and its coverage counts against the whole track, so the check flags the missing part (note it); the record keeps the stated figures. The legs are features of their own (kind `detour`, `by`), left out of the measured length, water and surfaces; the map draws the walk dotted in the trail's colour and a ride as a thin ink line with ties (a railway on a printed map), each in the legend only when present, and the note under the stage table says what is closed and the recommended way on. Downloads retry on dropped connections; `osm.fetch()` treats an Overpass `remark` with a runtime error (a timeout answered with status 200 and no elements) as a failure and retries instead of caching an empty result, and `pipeline/water.py` queries the bounding box of each piece of about 10 km of the route (`CHUNK`) and filters to 500 m itself (`WITHIN_M`; 250 m before 2026-11-01, issue #115: 250 m missed taps in villages just off the trail): Overpass's polyline `around` sometimes silently matched nothing (on 2026-10-29 it did for every route), and `osm.around()` drops repeated points, which can trip it too. The printed report flags stages where less than 95% of the track is within 30 m of the result: usually OSM is missing a path there; record it in the trail's `notes`.
- OSM data (for scaling to more trails, 2026-10-30): the steps ask `osm.source()` (pipeline/osm.py) four questions, ways near a track, a relation with its ways' geometry, water points in a box, the tags of given ways, and get Overpass-shaped JSON back, so their caches look the same whoever answered. By default `pipeline/osmlocal.py` answers from Geofabrik extracts: for each of the trail's points (GPX tracks, termini, stage ends) the smallest Geofabrik region holding it (`regions_for()`, from Geofabrik's index with region polygons), downloaded once into `~/.cache/huckepack/osm-extracts/` (re-downloaded with --refresh when older than 7 days; the dated file name is the extract's date); one pass over each extract (pyosmium, pinned) collects the corridor within 600 m of the points, highway ways with nodes and tags, the named route relations (with children) and their ways, water nodes, cached as git-ignored `data/osm/corridors/<slug>.json.gz` and rebuilt when it doesn't cover the guide points or lacks a named relation. Relation-only trails first collect just the relations' own ways (one pass), whose geometry then defines the corridor for a second pass (a box around a long trail's ends would hold far too much). The extracts come from the termini and stage `end` points only, so a relation-only trail spanning several regions needs a stage `end` in each (the Goldsteig's stage 7 end brings in the Oberpfalz); where relation member ways are still missing after the first pass (a route over a border), the regions around the found ways' ends are read too. A trail relation's own ferry ways (`route=ferry`, the Kungsleden's rowing boats) are in the matcher's graph; no other ferries get into the corridor. Motorways, construction and platforms stay out of the corridor; trunk roads only where OSM tags them for walkers (`foot=yes`, `designated`, `permissive`; `walkable()` in pipeline/osmlocal.py, the same filter in the Overpass query): the Offa's Dyke Path walks the A438 near Hay-on-Wye. A download that stops early resumes from where it stopped (HTTP Range on the dated file; Geofabrik's connections dropped often on 2026-10-31). The data carry the extract's date, which the caches record as retrieved. Checked against the Overpass results on the Laugavegur and Merano (2026-10-30): identical map lines, route ways and water; Merano's surface tags were stale in the Overpass cache (the step had kept tags from before stage 4 changed) and are now complete. `pipeline/all.py` runs every step in order (routes, water, surface, elevation, landcover, climate, access), drops the caches that depend on the map line when routes changed it (the stale-tags trap; not the climate downloads, which the climate step checks against the grid cells itself), stops at a failing step with the command to resume (except the climate step: when it fails, it leaves the trail pending, `pending()`, and the run goes on; `pipeline.climate --pending` catches up later, and the check flags it: don't publish a trail before its climate is in; before the open data, 2026-10-31, the historical API's daily quota allowed about six trails a day), and ends with `check()`: stages under 95 % matched, stage and route lengths more than 10 % off the map line, stated against terrain-model ascent, a dry stretch over 25 km, over 10 % unknown surface, stations over 300 km away or an airport's road over three times its straight line plus 50 km, no `dataCheck`, and a route above 1,500 m without `treeLine` or a "Tree line" note.
- Freshness check (issue #90): `.github/workflows/freshness.yml` runs `pipeline/freshness.py` on the 1st of every month (and by hand). It changes nothing in the records. It checks every link in a record (sources, website, managing bodies, permit, stage pages) for errors and redirects, re-reads every stage page the Outdooractive reader can read and compares the figures with the record's beyond rounding (distance > 0.15 km and 2 %, climbs and time > 10 and 5 %; only a stage's own page: the Rennsteig gives every stage the whole route's), and lists sources whose `retrieved` is over a year old. Redirects to the same page are fine (a home page picking its language, a language prefix, an Outdooractive short link growing its title: the page's last path segment or query id still in the new address); a deep link landing on the home page, or stage pages landing on one page together, is broken. Broken also: 4xx/5xx, no such host, a certificate that fails. Not checked, only listed: 401/403/429 (never worked around), resets, timeouts and TLS too old for the checker (never weakened). With more than 30 % of sites silent it stops, the network being down. - Elevation (`pipeline/elevation.py`): samples the main stages every 100 m (every 500 m on trails over 1,000 km, `spacing_for()`, so their pages stay near 1 MB; the derived `spacingM` says which, and the page's texts and the gradient bars follow it) on the map line (same kilometres as water and surfaces), heights from GEDTM30 v1.2 (the user's decision of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md: OpenGeoHub's global ensemble digital terrain model, bare earth, 30 m grid, CC BY 4.0, source `gedtm30`; Ho et al. 2025, PeerJ 13:e19673, data doi 10.5281/zenodo.18887460). It is one global Float32 COG (`DTM_URL`, 432 GB, no account, nothing downloaded whole), read remotely with rasterio in windows around chunks of 40 consecutive samples with a 2-pixel margin (`windows()`; `GDAL_ENV`: no directory listing, retries, a larger block cache), interpolated bilinearly between the four nearest cell centres (`bilinear()`, cells without data left out; a sample with none, open sea, is taken as 0 m and the run says so); values are metres above EGM2008 (ignore the file's `scale=0.1` tag); it covers 62°S–83.7°N, every trail so far inside. A trail takes about 15 s. The sampled heights are cached in `data/raw/<slug>/elevation-gedtm30.json`. The climbs always come from samples every 100 m (`CLIMB_SPACING_M`; the user's decision of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md): the derived `stageClimbs` (each main stage's unrounded up and down by `stage_climbs()`, threshold as below) and `climbSpacingM`, written for every trail, from the profile's own samples where they are 100 m apart, else from a second sampling every 100 m for the climbs alone (`route_samples(slug, 100)`, cached in `elevation-gedtm30-100.json`; the trails over 1,000 km keep their 500 m profile for the page, but 500 m samples had missed 25–58 % of their climbs); `terrainClimb()`, `stageEstimates()` and `routeEstimate()` take them, summing the profile's samples only for a derived file from before, and `pipeline.all`'s check compares them (`model_ascent()`). Side trips the record walks there and back (`outAndBack` variants) are sampled every 100 m along their own map line (`side_trips()`, `side_trip_climbs()`, cached in `elevation-gedtm30-sidetrips.json`), one way, into the derived `sideTrips`. Before 2026-11-07 the Copernicus DEM GLO-30, a surface model that measured the treetops in forests (raw climb sums +22 % in the median against 34 official ascents). GEDTM30 is derived from GLO-30 (and AW3D30); since no figure comes from GLO-30 any more, the `copdem` source left the records and the Copernicus WorldDEM-30 notice and liability disclaimer left the legal notice (the user's decision, 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md); GEDTM30's CC BY citation covers the heights. The other Copernicus notices stay: ERA5 (climate, "contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information") and ESA WorldCover (land cover, "Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data"). **Ascent and descent summed from the terrain model count with a 10 m threshold** (`CLIMB_THRESHOLD_M` and `climbs()` in `src/lib/trails.ts`, the same in `pipeline/all.py`'s check): a climb counts only once the height has risen 10 m above the last reference height, a drop likewise, and the reference moves there; per stage along the stage's own samples (`stageEstimates()`, each stage starts fresh), over the whole route for trails without stages (`routeEstimate()`) and the difficulty's climbing per km; gradients, steepness and the profile drawing keep the raw heights. With it the estimates sit at 0 % in the median against the 34 official ascents (mean gap 8 %). National lidar models (Saxony DGM1, Czech DMR 5G) are candidates in docs/data-sources.md, not in use: one profile must not mix terrain models. After rerunning this step, rerun pipeline.landcover (its highest forest sample takes the profile's height) and pipeline.climate (its places are the highest and lowest point). The page shows it in the Terrain section (below). Its heights come from the terrain model and are not mixed with the stated stage elevations or ascent.

- Regions (`pipeline/regions.py`, the user's rule of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md; also a step of `pipeline.all`, after landcover): besides countries and continents the searches group trails by regions from open data, never hand-picked: first-level subdivisions (`admin`: Natural Earth's "Admin 1 – States, Provinces", 1:10m, public domain, ISO 3166-2 codes; where Natural Earth's polygons are a level below ISO's first level they are joined by the property naming it, `DISSOLVE`: France's departments into regions, Italy's provinces into regions, Spain's provinces into autonomous communities, the UK's districts into England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, Belgium's provinces into its regions) and natural regions (`natural`: Natural Earth's "Geography regions" polygons, ranges, plateaus, peninsulas, deserts, islands, with their class `cla`; without continents and without the largest islands and plains, scale rank 2 or less: Great Britain, Ireland, Iceland, the British Isles, the North European Plain; English names where Natural Earth's English name is the same place, `natural_name()`). Names are English (the user's decisions of 2026-11-07): an admin region takes its Wikidata item's English label (CC0; by Natural Earth's `wikidataid`, else its ISO 3166-2 code, P300, or HASC code, P8119; `english_names()`, cached in `~/.cache/huckepack/naturalearth/wikidata-en.json`), "Cataluña" → Catalonia, "Toscana" → Tuscany, Natural Earth's name kept in the entry's `also`; a natural region likewise (2026-11-07, the user's decision): its item's English label where Natural Earth's label or English name is among the item's labels and aliases in any language (`needs_search()`, "Erzgebirge" → Ore Mountains), else the first landform (P31 in `NATURAL_TYPES`) a Wikidata search for the label finds (`search_landform()`; the Grampian Mountains, whose linked item is a single hill), with `NAME_FIX` for the four Natural Earth or Wikidata get wrong (its Appennini polygon is the whole range → Apennines, not the Ligurian Apennines its English name and item say; Columbia Mountains, Mantiqueira Mountains, Bohemian Forest); an admin and a natural region that are one place, each covering at least 90 % of the other's area (`MERGE_OVERLAP`, counted on a 200 × 200 grid over both, cos-latitude weighted, `overlap()`; checked for the pairs a trail has a fifth of its route in each), are one entry under the admin region's English name, else the natural one's (Corse and the island of Corsica → "Corsica", also "Corse"); one name is listed once per trail (the Balearic Islands: the region and the island group within it). The large natural regions (Iberian Peninsula, Scandinavia) stay. A trail is in a region when at least 20 % of its main route lies inside (`SHARE_MIN`, the landscape filter's rule): the main stages of `public/routes/<slug>.geojson` cut into pieces of at most 500 m, each counted by its midpoint (ray casting with numpy, no GIS library). The two layers (v5.1.2, GeoJSON from Natural Earth's repository) are downloaded once into `~/.cache/huckepack/naturalearth/`. Writes only the derived `regions` key ([{name, kind, code?, cla?, also?, share}], the largest share first), so it merges cleanly with the other steps; the search record carries the other names as `placeAlso`. 132 regions hold the 131 trails (2026-11-07: Alps 9, England 17, Scotland 9, Appalachian Mountains 8, Rocky Mountains 5, Patagonia 3, Pyrenees 1, the Carros de Foc; the Camino Francés crosses the Pyrenees in under 20 %). The record's own hand-written `regions` stay what the trail page shows; the searches use the derived ones (`searchSummary()`'s `places`). Rerun it when a map line changes.

- Links (the user's decision of 2026-11-07, docs/scope.md): every link to another website opens in a new tab with `rel="noopener"` (never `noreferrer`: operators see huckepack.me, the origin only, as referrer; no referrer meta overrides the browsers' default) plus a screen-reader note in `.sr-only`; links within huckepack stay in the same tab. Followed by default; `nofollow` only for commercial businesses cited as sources that aren't the trail's managing or official body, per URL prefix in `data/reference/commercial-links.yaml` (each with its reason; a park's booking page or a tourism board's own Outdooractive-run portal counts as official), and for the per-place links made in the browser (the chart tooltips' and lists' `osm-link`, the map's place popups). ## Ground rules

- Only official trails: a route designated or maintained by a government body or a recognised trail association. Unofficial routes (for example the Hayduke Trail) are out of scope unless docs/scope.md says otherwise. A hut circuit run by its huts counts when it has its own official website; its `managedBy` names who runs it (docs/scope.md).
- Every figure on a page must come from a source listed in the trail's record (or, for sources used across trails, in docs/data-sources.md), with its licence and retrieval date. Check a source's licence before using it: official GPX tracks are often copyrighted, and OpenStreetMap data requires ODbL attribution.
- Trail lengths, elevation gain and similar numbers differ between sources. Show which source a number comes from rather than silently picking one, and never mix them in one figure.

## Working rules

Three rules for all future development (set 2026-10-29), on top of the mission and the ground rules:

1. **Bigger design changes start as a preview with three approaches, switched inline on the real pages.** A bigger design change is a new section or chart, a reworked page layout or navigation, a new page type, or a redesign of an existing component (not text edits, bug fixes, small spacing tweaks, or contrast and accessibility fixes). Build at least three clearly different approaches (more when the user asks, e.g. "from conservative to crazy"), on real data, into the real page(s) themselves rather than a separate page, and add `src/components/PreviewSwitch.astro` to the page(s) concerned: a small switch fixed at the bottom left ("Now · A · B · C"), each approach with a one-line idea and its trade-off shown beside the switch. Where a preview has two independent dimensions (e.g. design and text, issue #210), use one switch per dimension with `stacked`, inside one fixed container the page places, so the user can combine them. It sets `<html data-preview-<topic>="a">` and remembers the choice per topic in localStorage (key `preview:<topic>`, restored before the first paint by Base's head script, since a component in the body can't add a CSP-hashed inline script), so it holds across pages and reloads; each approach styles itself off that attribute (CSS `:global(html[data-preview-<topic>="a"])` …) or reads it in its script (a `previewchange` event fires on a switch). "Now" shows the page as it is, for comparison. Work on a local branch `preview/<topic>`, check every approach in both themes and at phone width, and give the user the local URL with a short table: each approach, its idea, its trade-off. Then ask, per approach or per part, whether to keep, change or remove it, and iterate on the branch. Preview branches are never pushed (everything on `main` goes live). Once the user picks, build only the chosen design into the page without the switch or the other approaches, and delete the preview branch. Flat colours only in proposals (no gradients or textures). Before 2026-11-01 previews were separate pages under `src/pages/preview/`; issue #3 (Landscape) was the first model, the home page's hero (2026-11-01) the model for the inline switch.
2. **Every new trail's data is double-checked before it is reported done** (docs/trail-building.md, step 6). Preferred: confirm each figure (length, ascent and descent, days, season, stage distances, times and heights, termini, difficulty, sleeping and camping rules, and the region's tree line where the route might reach it) against a second, independent source (e.g. the tourism board, a national park, a national trail portal, a guidebook publisher's facts). Figures may differ by at most 10 %; a larger difference is recorded in the trail's `notes` and `dataCheck` with both values and named to the user; the page keeps the official figure (docs/scope.md). Derived figures that have an official counterpart (map-line length vs. stated length, highest point vs. stated) are checked the same way. Where no second source exists: build the site and check the figures as the trail page shows them, one by one, against the original source (catches typos and wrong conversions in the record). Either way, add a note to the record ("Data check (date): …" with what was compared and what agrees) and fill its `dataCheck` field: `date`, `against` (the second sources), `pageAgainst` (sources the built page was checked against where no second source exists) and every `deviations` entry over 10 % (`figure`, `ours`, `theirs`, `source`). The check is internal quality assurance: nothing of it is shown on the page, and a source used only for the check gets `checkOnly: true`, so the page's Sources list leaves it out. Give the user the comparison as a table. When a second source publishes a figure the first lacks (e.g. stage times), use it with its own source rather than our estimate (`stages.timesSource`, named under the stage table). All three trails were checked on 2026-10-29.
3. **What explains a chart sits behind an (i).** Sources, methods and reading notes for a graph are not caption lines under it: they go into a popover behind a small (i) icon in the chart's top-right corner (at the end of the legend row where the chart has one on top, else in a slim row of its own above the drawing; `<figcaption class="chart-info">`, absolutely placed with `<Explain icon term="About this chart" title="…">`; `Explain.svelte` has the same `icon` prop), to keep the page calm. The chart itself keeps what is needed to read it at a glance: its legend or key, axis labels and its accessible name. Labels that explain a figure stay dotted Explain terms.

- Inline charts stay small (2026-10-30: the home page went from 1.2 MB to 0.4 MB of HTML, the long trail pages about 30 % down): `src/lib/svg.ts` rounds chart coordinates to a tenth of a unit (`n1`; the trail page's `xAt`, `yAt` and `laneX` return rounded values), draws a surface strip as one path per category (`stripPaths`, runs of the same category joined by `mergeRuns`; TrailSpark and the trail page's ground strip), and the trail page's elevation slices are one path of rectangles per colour (`.slice.up`/`.slice.down` with `--p` in steps of 10 %, the colour mix in the CSS; `rectsPath()` writes each rectangle relative to the one before), clipped to the area under the profile, whose outline (thinned by `simplify()` to what shows at the chart's scale, 0.2 units) is drawn once and used both as the clip and, clipped to the profile's box, as the line; bars narrower than half a unit (long trails) are drawn per half-unit column, up or down by the column's balance and as steep as its bars on average (2026-11-06: the Alpe-Adria chart 307 → 76 KB, the picture the same). New charts with many marks should do the same: one path per colour, not one element per mark. The hover scripts read their data from one `<script type="application/json" class="chart-data">` block per chart (`jsonBlock()` in the trail page, compact tuples, `<` escaped), not from HTML-escaped data attributes (the Alpe-Adria page went from 1.37 MB to 0.76 MB with this and the landscape change); what is large (the terrain's samples, bars and runs, the landscape runs, every place in the water and sleeping lanes) goes in a second block, `class="chart-heavy"`, which `src/integrations/chart-files.mjs` moves after the build into `/trails/<slug>/charts.json` (one array in the blocks' order; the block keeps `data-src` and `data-i`), fetched once on the first pointer, touch or focus near any such chart (`heavyData()`, `loadOnApproach()` in the page's script; in dev the blocks stay full) (2026-11-06: the Alpe-Adria page 943 → 391 KB with the slices change); the landscape lanes are one path per group (`stripPaths`) with one tooltip at the pointer (landscape, km, stage), like the terrain chart's, instead of a title per run. Astro scopes component styles with a class (`scopedStyleStrategy: "class"` in astro.config.mjs; same specificity as the default attribute, less markup), so a script must never overwrite an element's whole `class`; use `classList`. Distances on the globe are `src/lib/geo.ts` (`kmBetween` on [lon, lat], `roundDistance`), shared by the home page and the trail map.
