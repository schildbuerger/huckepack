# Mission

> **huckepack helps outdoor enthusiasts find the long-distance trail that suits them, and plan
> the journey with the data that matters on the way: route and stages, climbs and ground
> underfoot, landscape and climate, water, beds and getting there. Gathered from official and
> open sources, named on every page, and comparable from one trail to the next.**

Every product decision starts here: a feature, a trail, a data source or a design change belongs
on huckepack if it makes finding a trail or planning one better, and doesn't if it doesn't.

## Who it's for

People who hike for days or weeks: thru-hikers and section hikers, from a first hut-to-hut week
to a five-month trail. They are often still choosing ("which trail, and when?") or preparing
("what will each day be like, and what must I carry?"). They want facts they can rely on, not
inspiration alone.

## The two jobs

1. **Find a trail that fits.** By when they can go, how long and how hard, where, how they want
   to sleep, how much water they can carry, what they want underfoot. The search page and the
   guided "Help me choose" answer this; every filter is a planning question.
2. **Enrich the planning of the journey.** Once a trail is chosen, its page gathers what usually
   takes a dozen open tabs, in one place and on one set of kilometres.

## The aspects of planning

A trail page grows along the questions a long-distance hiker asks. Today:

| Aspect | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Route and stages | Where does it go, and how is it split into days? |
| Distance, ascent, difficulty | How long and how hard is it? |
| Elevation and steepness | Where are the big climbs and the steep bits? |
| Ground underfoot | Paths, gravel or tarmac? |
| Landscape | Forest, open hills, rock, snow? |
| Season, climate, daylight | When can I go, and what weather do I pack for? |
| Water | How far between drinking water? |
| Sleeping and wild camping | Huts, campsites, rooms? Can I pitch a tent? |
| Getting there | How do I reach the start? |

Candidates that fit the mission (ranked in [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)): resupply and shops along
the way, permits and fees, costs, mobile coverage, hazards (river crossings, exposure, wildlife),
public transport at stage ends, and comparisons across trails.

## Principles

- **Data, not opinions.** Figures from official and open sources, presented so they can be
  compared. No ratings, no "best of" lists, no marketing text.
- **Every figure names its source.** Where sources disagree, show which number comes from where;
  never mix them in one figure. Our own estimates are marked as estimates (≈) and say how they
  were made.
- **Comparable across trails.** The same measures, scales and charts on every trail page (one
  height scale on the search cards, the SAC scale for difficulty, the same landscape groups), so
  one trail can be read against another.
- **Relevant to planning.** A figure earns its place if it changes a decision: which trail,
  which month, how many days, what to carry, where to sleep.
- **Official trails.** Routes designated or maintained by a government body or a recognised trail
  association; see the ground rules in CLAUDE.md.
- **Calm and accessible.** Clear on a phone and in dark mode, readable for colour-blind readers
  and screen readers, quick to load; explanations on demand, not in the way.

## What huckepack is not

- Not a booking site, a tour operator or a shop.
- Not a navigation app: it shows the route, it doesn't replace a map, a GPX file or the trail's
  own guidance on the ground.
- Not a community or review site (for now): no user ratings, trip reports or comments.
- Not a substitute for the official sources: it links to them and says when their figures were
  checked.

## Deciding on something new

Ask, in this order:

1. Does it help someone **find** a trail that suits them, or **plan** the journey better?
2. Which planning question does it answer, and would the answer change a decision?
3. Is there an **official or open source** for it, with a licence that allows using it, and can
   every figure name that source?
4. Can it be shown the **same way on every trail**, so trails compare?
5. Does it keep the pages **calm, accessible and fast**?

If the first answer is no, it doesn't belong on huckepack, however nice it looks. If a later
answer is no, find another way or another source before building it.
